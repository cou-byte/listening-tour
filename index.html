Listening to Florida, from the Panhandle to the Keys

What the tour is

The 2026-27 Lastinger Listening Tour brings people across Florida into conversations about how they access, use, and share education data and information. Through interviews, focus groups, and a statewide survey, we are learning what works, where people encounter challenges, and what they would like to see improved. We will review what we learn with participants and use it to develop practical recommendations for education data access and use in Florida.

This is a listening and research effort, not an audit, a compliance review, or a technology sales pitch.

The Listening Tour is supported by the Helios Education Foundation.

How we listen

At each regional stop, we invite people with different roles and experiences to take part in interviews and small group conversations. We use a common set of questions, adapted for each audience, to learn how people access and use education data and information in their work and decision-making.

    Add your voice

    The tour can only visit ten regions, but the statewide survey is open to anyone who collects, receives, shares, or uses education data or information in Florida. It takes about 10–15 minutes, responses are anonymous and reported only in summary, and there are no right or wrong answers. What you share will shape the recommendations that come from this work.

    What we're listening for

    We’re asking people across Florida:

    What people use today

    What education data and information they currently use and value.

    What’s missing

    What is missing, difficult to access, confusing, or untimely.

    Where burden builds

    Where systems, processes, and reporting create burden or duplication.

    What makes it useful

    What makes information useful, understandable, trustworthy, secure, and actionable.

    How AI is used

    How AI is being used with education data and what opportunities or concerns people see.

    What could change

    What changes could better support teaching, learning, programs, research, and decision-making.

    Who we set out to hear

    Thirteen audience groups, each with its own facilitation module and session goal. Students are represented through families, caregivers, and the educators closest to them rather than engaged directly in this phase.

    Where we are in the work
